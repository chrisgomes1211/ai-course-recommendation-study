Welcome! If you've ever stood in front of a blank canvas and thought "I'd love to try this, but I wouldn't know where to start" — this little course was made for you. Oil painting has a reputation for being intimidating: the solvents, the mediums, the centuries of technique. We take all of that seriously, and then we take it apart gently, one small step at a time.

Over about 14 hours of video, we move through the basics without rushing: how to set up a workspace that doesn't take over your kitchen table, what those tubes of paint actually are, how colors mix (and why gray is the most useful color you'll ever learn), and how to finish a painting you're genuinely happy to hang on a wall. There are no prerequisites here except curiosity.

A note on pace: Some people finish the whole course in a month; others take a year, dipping in and out around work and family. Both are completely fine. There are no deadlines and nothing expires — the course waits for you.

A few of the things you'll paint

We start small and stay friendly — single objects, simple scenes, the kind of subject you can set up on a windowsill:

  • A lemon, an apple, and a crumpled cloth (the classic still life)
  • Mixing a whole painting from just three colors
  • Skies — the fastest way to learn soft edges
  • Your morning coffee, painted from life
  • A simple landscape from a photograph you took
  • Brushwork drills that feel more like doodling than practice
  • Loose flowers without counting petals
  • Knowing when a painting is done, and signing it
About the materials

You don't need much to begin: a small set of paints (we recommend starting with six tubes), two or three brushes, a palette, and something to paint on. A proper kit costs less than most textbooks. Inside the course there's a shopping list with three budget levels — "under $50", "the sensible middle", and "treat yourself" — so you can start where you are.

On solvents: yes, they exist, and no, you don't need them to start. We show a low-odor, no-solvent setup you can use in a small apartment, plus how to ventilate properly if you'd rather work the traditional way.

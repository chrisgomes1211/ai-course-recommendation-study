🧪 The experiment in 60 seconds

  1. A model is shown all five course pages as raw HTML, order shuffled every trial (controls position bias).
  2. It must recommend exactly one course and justify the pick.
  3. In the same conversation, it then answers an 11-question exit survey: how influential was price, reviews, credentials, badges… (1–5 scale).
  4. Repeat 30× per model per page-condition → 1,041 complete trials, CSV-logged with costs, tokens and latencies.

Each model is near-deterministic (temperature 0.1) — but models disagree with each other. The aggregate is balanced because heuristics differ, not because any model hesitates. Canvas's and Creative's single strengths (review volume; gentle pitch) never beat credentials or price in any model.

What the models say vs. what they do

"Self-reports track revealed choices closely — Artistry choosers rate credentials 4.99/5 and price 3.30; Palette choosers rate price 5.00/5 and credentials 2.51. Stated reasons are a trustworthy window into LLM decision-making."

Across all 1,041 exit surveys, stated driver importance: Description 4.61 ≈ Reviews 4.56 > Clarity 4.41 > Price 4.03 ≈ Comparison table 3.99 ≈ Credentials 3.96 > Social proof 3.10 > Badge 2.61 > Payment plan 2.24 > Anchoring 2.00. The classic CRO levers — badges, strike-through anchors, installment plans — barely register.

💡 Surprises & what's next

  • Surprise 1: balance comes entirely from models disagreeing with each other — no model was ever "undecided". Temperature-driven uncertainty ≈ 0.
  • Surprise 2: review volume — the strongest social signal on the Canvas page (260 reviews) — never won a single trial for 6 of 7 models.
  • Surprise 3: gpt-4.1-mini was the only model producing genuine 5-way variety (picked all five pages) — smaller ≠ simpler heuristics.
  • Next (Run 3): raise Canvas's rating floor 4.3★ → 4.6★ and re-run all models as a separate condition — a causal test of whether heavy-signal strength (rating) is what unlocks review-volume wins.

⚠️ Limits

Cost: ~$47.75 total across both runs (cap $100). Models: 7 low-tier models, one per provider, near-deterministic by design — findings characterize this tier, not frontier models. Pages: fictional courses and synthetic reviews; real brands may behave differently. 9 of 1,050 planned trials were lost to transient API errors.
